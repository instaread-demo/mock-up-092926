Best Family-Friendly Campgrounds for RVs, Tents and Camping With Kids

Whether your family is looking for simple pleasures or big-time thrills, these RV parks are sure to please even the pickiest little camper.

By Amanda Adler

The right family friendly campground depends on who is coming and how you camp. Younger kids may do better with easy recreation and nearby restrooms, while older kids may care more about swimming, hiking, biking, or other activities. RV families also need to think about site size, hookups, and road access, while tent campers may care more about shade, campsite layout, and facilities.

This guide compares family camping options based on both the experience they offer and practical campsite fit, including amenities, activities, RV or tent suitability, reservations, and other details that can affect the trip.

While reading, you can start finding family-friendly campgrounds near you on Campendium. Compare campground listings, photos, amenities, and recent camper reviews on the map before choosing where to stay.

TL;DR

  • Best for younger kids and first-time family campers: Look for developed campgrounds with restrooms, easy recreation, playgrounds or beaches, and activities close to the campsite.
  • Best for RV families: Compare family amenities alongside site length, hookups, road access, dump stations and other RV-specific details.
  • Best way to choose: Check recent camper reviews, photos, campground amenities and nearby alternatives before making a reservation.

What Makes a Campground Family-Friendly?

A family-friendly campground is one that fits the ages of the kids, the camping setup, and the kind of trip the family wants. A playground may matter for one family, while another may care more about lake access, easy trails, clean restrooms, or enough room for an RV.

Here are some of the main features worth comparing before you book:

A campground does not need every feature on this list to work well for families. The better choice is the one that matches your kids, your rig or tent setup, and what you want to do during the stay.

Best Family-Friendly Campgrounds at a Glance

Family-friendly camping can look very different from one trip to another. This shortlist mixes national and state park campgrounds with a private campground, plus options centered on beaches, hiking, wildlife, water recreation, and developed amenities. It also keeps RV and tent fit in the comparison rather than judging campgrounds on activities alone.

Campground facilities and reservation rules can change, and RV fit often varies by individual site or loop. Check the current campground listing, recent camper reviews, and the managing agency before booking, especially when traveling with a larger rig.

The Best Family-Friendly Campgrounds

These campgrounds cover several kinds of family trips, from beach weekends and national park stays to full-hookup RV camping with activities close to the campsite. The best fit depends on what your family wants to do and how much campground infrastructure you need.

Fort De Soto Park Campground, Florida

Best for: Families who want beaches, biking, and water recreation close to camp.

Fort De Soto combines a developed campground with beaches, playgrounds, fishing, kayaking, and a paved multi-use trail. RV and tent sites have water and electric service, and campground facilities include showers, laundry, restrooms, and dump stations. Reservations are available, with pet camping limited to designated sites. 

Recent Campendium campers mention kayaking from waterfront sites, biking to the historic fort, and spending time at the beaches. Availability can be the harder part of planning a stay, and a 2026 reviewer noted that some bathroom facilities felt dated.

Myrtle Beach State Park Campground, South Carolina

Best for: Families who want an ocean beach with developed campground facilities.

The campground sits within walking distance of the beach and offers playgrounds, swimming, fishing, and trails. Families can choose among tent sites, water-and-electric sites, and full-hookup RV sites, with hot showers, restrooms, and laundry available. Reservations are accepted.

Recent Campendium campers commonly mention the easy beach access and shaded campsites. Site size and hookup placement can vary, and some reviewers report aircraft noise from the nearby airport or extra noise during major local events.

Big Meadows Campground, Virginia

Best for: Families who want Shenandoah hiking, wildlife, and park programs close to camp.

Big Meadows puts families near the Byrd Visitor Center, Dark Hollow Falls, and the meadow itself. Both tents and RVs are allowed, with potable water, flush toilets, a dump station, laundry, and paid showers available seasonally. There are no campsite hookups, and reservations are required.

Recent Campendium campers mention deer sightings, convenient trail access, and generally clean facilities. RV families should look closely at site dimensions and the route through Shenandoah, since mountain driving and individual campsite fit can require more planning with a larger rig.

Elkmont Campground, Tennessee

Best for: Families who want creekside camping and hiking in Great Smoky Mountains National Park.

Elkmont offers tent and RV camping near Little River, with hiking access and the historic Elkmont area nearby. The campground has drinking water and flush toilets, but no hookups, showers, or on-site dump station. RVs are limited to 35 feet and trailers to 32 feet, and reservations are required. 

Recent Campendium campers mention riverfront sites, walks directly from the campground, and the sound of the river at night. Some also describe sites as close together, so families wanting more privacy should check the campground map and recent photos before selecting a site.

Indiana Dunes State Park Campground, Indiana

Best for: Families who want Lake Michigan beach time with easy campground facilities.

The campground has electric campsites along with access to hiking, a playground, and the Lake Michigan shoreline. Showers, restrooms, and a dump station make it a practical choice for both tent campers and RV families. Campsites can be reserved through Indiana State Parks.

Recent Campendium campers mention easy access to the beach, paved or concrete camping pads, and the chance to hike directly into the dunes. Sites do not have individual water or sewer hookups, and individual layouts vary, so RV travelers should check photos and site dimensions before booking.

Normandy Farms Campground, Massachusetts

Best for: Families with younger kids who want plenty to do without leaving the campground.

Normandy Farms is the most activity-heavy option on this list. Families have access to pools, a splash pad, playgrounds, an indoor recreation area, sports facilities, and a fishing pond. Tent camping and full-hookup RV sites are available, along with showers, laundry, and other developed campground facilities. 

Recent Campendium campers mention clean facilities, organized activities, well-kept sites, and pools that give kids plenty to do. The tradeoff is a busier campground atmosphere and higher costs than many public campgrounds, with some reviewers also noting crowd and noise levels during busy stays.

Game Lodge Campground, South Dakota

Best for: Families interested in wildlife, ranger programs, and Custer State Park activities.

Game Lodge Campground sits near the Custer State Park Visitor Center and Peter Norbeck Outdoor Education Center. Grace Coolidge Creek runs through the campground toward a swimming beach and playground, giving families several activities close to their campsite. Tent and RV camping are available, including electric sites, along with showers and other campground facilities during the main operating season.

A 2026 Campendium reviewer specifically reported that their kids enjoyed the ranger programs offered at the campground. RV travelers also mention manageable access and level sites. Full hookups are not available, and some services can vary outside the main season.

Chatfield State Park Campground, Colorado

Best for: RV families who want full hookups and lake recreation near Denver.

Chatfield combines a large developed campground with boating, swimming, trails, and other activities around the reservoir. The campground has tent sites and RV sites, including full-hookup and pull-through options, along with showers, laundry, and a dump station. Reservations are required.

Recent Campendium campers mention spacious sites, clean bathhouses, and good options for larger RVs, although shade varies considerably. Taller RVs should also pay attention to access: the Deer Creek entrance has a 13-foot height restriction, so some rigs need to use the Plum Creek entrance instead.

Watchman Campground, Utah

Best for: Families planning to spend most of their time exploring Zion National Park.

Watchman is next to the Zion Canyon Visitor Center, making the park shuttle, nearby trails, and Springdale relatively easy to reach without moving the RV every day. Both tents and RVs are accommodated, and some sites have electric service. There are no full hookups or campground showers, but a dump station is available. Reservations are required. 

Recent Campendium campers repeatedly mention the convenient walk to the visitor center and shuttle system. RV travelers with larger rigs should check both campsite dimensions and current Zion road restrictions before choosing their route into the park.

Upper Pines Campground, California

Best for: Families who want to stay inside Yosemite Valley near major trails and park transportation.

Upper Pines places campers within walking or biking distance of many Yosemite Valley trailheads and services, with free shuttle stops nearby. It accommodates tents and RVs and provides potable water, flush toilets, and a dump station. There are no hookups or showers. Reservations are required year-round and can be difficult to secure for busy dates.

Recent Campendium campers mention the ability to walk to places such as Mirror Lake and the Mist Trail and use the shuttle for other valley stops. The main drawbacks are close campsite spacing, heavy demand, and limits on which sites can accommodate larger RVs and trailers.

Fort Stevens State Park, Oregon

Best for: Families who want biking, beach access, history, and several campground amenities in one park.

Fort Stevens has an unusually broad mix of family activities, including Coffenbury Lake, miles of multi-use trails, beach access, a historic military site, and the Peter Iredale shipwreck. Camping choices include full-hookup RV sites, electric-and-water sites, tent sites, cabins, and yurts. Hot showers and a dump station are also available, and reservations are required. 

Recent Campendium campers mention biking and walking trails, paddling, historical sites, and plenty to explore with kids. The campground is large, and experiences can differ by loop, including cell reception and site condition. Check current Oregon State Parks notices because infrastructure work may temporarily affect some loops.

Cranberry Lake Campground, Washington

Best for: Families who want forest camping with beaches, paddling, and hiking at Deception Pass.

Cranberry Lake gives families access to swimming, fishing, paddling, and a large trail network within Deception Pass State Park. Both tents and RVs are accommodated, with electric and water service available at some sites along with restrooms and showers. Reservations are available and are worth making early for popular dates. 

Recent Campendium campers mention wooded sites, hiking, lake activities, and generally clean facilities. RV Camping With Kids: What to Check Before Booking

Family RV trips tend to put more pressure on campsite logistics. A site that technically allows RVs may still be a poor match for your rig, and missing water or power can matter more when several people are sharing the space.

  • RV length and campsite fit: “RV allowed” does not mean every site works for every rig. Check site length, parking configuration, turning room, and any size limits before booking.
  • Hookups: Electric, water, and sewer service can make longer family stays easier, especially when you are running appliances or using more water.
  • Dump stations and potable water: Confirm whether either is available at the campground or nearby, especially for multi-night stays without full hookups.
  • Road access: Read access notes for narrow roads, tight campground loops, steep grades, rough surfaces, or approaches that may be difficult with a trailer or larger motorhome.
  • Cell coverage: Check reported service if you need to work from camp, coordinate with family members, or keep a communication option available. Signal can vary by carrier, device, terrain, and campsite.
  • Recent RV reviews: Look for comments from campers using a similar RV type or length. Their notes can reveal tight turns, uneven sites, hookup placement, road conditions, and other details that basic campground listings may not show.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a campground family-friendly?

A family-friendly campground has features that fit the ages of your kids and the way your family camps. Useful signs include clean restrooms, easy recreation, playgrounds or water access, picnic areas, suitable RV or tent sites, and activities close enough to enjoy without spending much of the day driving.

What is the best campground in the U.S. for kids?

There is no single best campground for every family. Families with younger kids may prefer developed campgrounds with playgrounds and restrooms. RV families may want hookups and larger sites. For water recreation, look for beaches or lakes. For hiking and nature, consider campgrounds near easy trails, visitor centers, or ranger programs.

What amenities should I look for when camping with kids?

Useful amenities can include restrooms, showers, playgrounds, picnic tables, fire rings, swimming areas, beaches, easy trails, and other recreation close to camp. Families traveling with pets should also check the campground’s pet policy. The right mix depends on your children’s ages and how much time you expect to spend at the campground.

Do family-friendly campgrounds have RV hookups?

Some do, but family-friendly does not automatically mean RV hookups are available. Public campgrounds may have no hookups or electric-only sites, while private campgrounds and some state parks may offer water, electric, or full hookups. Check the specific campsite before reserving, since amenities can also vary within the same campground.

Do family campgrounds require reservations?

It depends on the campground, destination, managing agency, and time of year. Some accept reservations, some require them, and others may keep first-come, first-served sites. Check current booking rules and operating dates before your trip, especially for popular weekends, holidays, and high-demand destinations.
