Literariness Journal is pleased to invite original research articles for its First Anniversary Issue (Vol. 2, Issue 1), December 2026.

Marking the completion of our first year of publication, this issue welcomes scholarly contributions across literature, cultural studies, media studies, gender studies, digital humanities, and interdisciplinary approaches to the humanities.

We seek original and unpublished research that offers critical, theoretically grounded, and innovative perspectives on literary and cultural texts, emerging intellectual debates, and contemporary developments in the humanities. Contributions that engage with new methodologies, evolving cultural forms, emerging technologies, and underexplored areas of literary and cultural inquiry are particularly encouraged.

Literariness Journal subscription

The journal welcomes submissions from scholars at all stages of academic research and invites contributions that expand, challenge, or reimagine existing approaches to literary and cultural studies. Submissions addressing significant emerging areas of inquiry beyond the themes listed below are equally welcome.

Themes and Topics

  • Youth dissent, protest cultures, and the aesthetics of resistance
  • Gen Z language, slang, internet vernaculars, and emerging forms of communication
  • Caste, Dalit and Adivasi studies, Indigenous literatures, and marginalized epistemologies
  • Digital humanities, computational literary studies, distant reading, and data-driven criticism
  • Biopolitics, necropolitics, power, governance, surveillance, and contemporary cultural formations
  • Food studies, culinary cultures, fashion, dress, material culture, and everyday life
  • Urban cultures, spatial humanities, architecture, cities, and the politics of place
  • Public humanities, community scholarship, cultural institutions, and new modes of knowledge dissemination
  • Posthumanism, transhumanism, monster theory, and non-human subjectivities
  • Artificial worlds, synthetic realities, virtual existence, and simulated identities
  • AI, authorship, machine creativity, and the future of literary production
  • Attention economies, creator cultures, influencer culture, and mediated self-fashioning
  • Meme cultures, internet humor, participatory cultures, and vernacular creativity
  • Queer studies, trans studies, queer temporalities, and non-normative identities
  • Generative AI, algorithmic cultures, language models, and human–machine collaboration
  • AI ethics, algorithmic bias, automation, and the politics of technological power
  • Climate anxiety, eco-grief, emotional ecologies, and climate affect
  • Environmental justice, ecological displacement, and climate migration
  • Eco-criticism, climate fiction, eco-cinema, and environmental storytelling
  • Blue humanities, oceanic imaginaries, and hydrocolonial studies
  • Animal studies, multispecies ethics, and more-than-human humanities
  • Solarpunk, hopepunk, dystopian imaginaries, and alternative ecological futures
  • Speculative criticism, futurity studies, science fiction, and technological imaginaries
  • Digital literature, electronic textuality, hypertext, and new forms of reading and writing
  • Digital activism, networked publics, online resistance, and counter-publics
  • Platform cultures, surveillance, privacy, data politics, and digital citizenship
  • Misinformation, deepfakes, post-truth cultures, and the politics of authenticity
  • Digital intimacy, parasociality, online communities, and mediated relationships
  • Gaming narratives, interactive storytelling, player agency, and virtual worlds
  • Streaming cultures, OTT narratives, binge spectatorship, and changing screen cultures
  • Adaptation, transmedia storytelling, remediation, and media convergence
  • Cinema and literary studies: dialogue, influence, and intermediality
  • Film theory, spectatorship, visual culture, and cinematic representation
  • Contemporary cinema, independent cinema, world cinema, and alternative film cultures
  • Documentary cinema, archival film, testimony, and cultures of witnessing
  • Popular cinema, genre cultures, celebrity, stardom, and mass spectatorship
  • K-pop, global fandoms, fan cultures, popular music, and transnational youth cultures
  • Literary theory, critical theory, and new directions in contemporary criticism
  • Comparative literature, world literature, transnational literary cultures, and literary circulation
  • Contemporary fiction, poetry, drama, graphic narratives, and emerging literary forms
  • Genre studies, popular literature, crime fiction, romance, horror, fantasy, and speculative fiction
  • Life writing, autobiography, memoir, testimony, and self-narration
  • Gender, sexuality, embodiment, masculinities, femininities, and changing social identities
  • Disability studies, neurodivergence, crip theory, accessibility, and disability aesthetics
  • Body studies, fat studies, beauty cultures, reproductive politics, and mediated embodiment
  • Health humanities, illness narratives, mental health, care ethics, and cultures of vulnerability
  • Trauma, memory, affect, grief, and contemporary cultures of remembrance
  • Archives, counter-archives, cultural memory, heritage, and the politics of preservation
  • Diaspora, migration, exile, borders, refugee narratives, and transnational belonging
  • Decolonial studies, postcolonial studies, epistemic justice, and the politics of knowledge
  • Slow scholarship, academic precarity, open-access publishing, research ethics, and the future of scholarly communication
